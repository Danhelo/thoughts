## General Transfer:

**Cosmopolitan Problems Require Cosmopolitan Solutions**

I woke up in a cold sweat. The last nap in this long 14 hour trip was over. Briskly scrubbing my eyes open, I was filled with joy thinking of studying at Michigan State University. Grabbing my backpack, a hint of anxiety creeped in as I wondered what living in a small college town would be like. Here, I was going to have to learn the local’s way of life to fit in. 

  

I proceeded to navigate my way through the small airport, and headed to my dorm. I was happily surprised to see many international students in a state university, however, over the following weeks I noticed something odd. People from different cultures would not interact in the ways I had hoped. Honors students always hung out together regardless of nationality, which was great, but their conversations still revolved around local news. Last night’s game results, what high school you’re from, next weekend’s plans. I did not immediately find somewhere where I “fit in”. I made it my mission to investigate why.

  

My first conclusion was that this was not at all the fault of the institution. In fact, MSU has an overwhelming amount of cultural programs that work to build engaging communities. I discovered that the problem did not stem from a lack of interculturality. Instead, it seemed as if I could only connect with people that had a similar outlook on what college really is. Namely, not a place to have fun, but somewhere where you figure out how you want to help solve the world’s greatest problems. 

  

Armed with this insight, I was ready to test my new hypothesis. I decided to explore meeting a wide range of people, starting with a Greek fraternity. While I love having fun occasionally, I quickly found out that I do not know much about sports, and my priorities were misaligned with theirs, especially academically. So I dropped. This happened again and again with international and local cliques. I was losing hope of finding that elusive “fit in” feeling, but I loved all the new insights I was getting into myself and the world. 

  

One day, it finally clicked. I found the perfect niche in this forty thousand student campus thanks to the AI Club. Their members engaged me intellectually and loved talking about nerd things just as much as I did. However, they were also outgoing, ambitious, and had that cosmopolitan feel to them. My experiment helped me realize that I needed to be constantly talking about starting the next global business, the hypergraphical theory of everything, or the economic implications of worldwide mass automation, to “fit in”. Nonetheless, I only have this opportunity on Club board nights once a week. I learned that even though I have made loads of incredibly smart friends here, the scope of the problems they want to solve is different from mine. 

  

Contrast this with something as simple as the language used on Columbia’s website. “World’s most important centers of research” or  “Great Metropolis”. Now that’s starting to sound a lot like those AI Club conversations. And I know for a fact that’s the case on campus, thanks to my various visits there and the friendships I maintain with students. Things like laughing on the phone with Pablo’s (a Columbian senior) analysis of the international banking industry has made me excited at the prospect of finding more “AI Clubs” in a place like Columbia. A place where globally-minded conversations are a lot more common. Not to mention, I really want my next plane ride back on campus to be less stressful (and shorter!).
