Have the [[altruist currency]] follow this logic:

Traditional [[Capitalism]] determines profit by increasing capital. The only objective is to always accumulate more capital. While this drives short term growth, long term it leads to market inefficiencies as incentive structures for capital aggregation permeate the political system, and everything becomes capitalismified. 

What if the proxy for the change in value instead of being change in capital (the delta of perceived value of a thing), is the difference between the "real value", as dictated by *pure* market forces, and the *nominal* value, as dictated by current market conditions.

In other words, if we design a smart currency (maybe thru the use of crypto), we can program it in such a way that it increases in value only when the available market prices come closer to the true maximum efficiency prices for the economy.

The logic goes something like this:

I am a high income earner and thus can afford to pay a little bit more for an apple. Max I would pay is $4.50. An apple costs $3 to make. If I pay $4.50, a lot of producers will be incentivized to capture that profit and so a request will be sent to the crypto ledger: Daniel - $4.50 - Apple. A client will fulfill such request (aka a traditional market exchange)

However, part of the profit (an adjustable per commodity percentage) the producer made on that sale will automatically be deducted in a form of "tax". This tax will be used to automatically subsidize other requests - say my poor friend Tom that can only afford $2.75  for an apple, gets a $0.50 subsidy, and thus some producer fulfils his request to capture that profit. 

That's not where it ends, why would I want to subsidize a random stranger's purchase? Well, for every dollar I overpay, I get a stake in any of my subsidee's future payments.

The example would go something like this: I overpayed 1.50 for an apple. 1 dollar went to the company and I got my apple. 50 cents went to Tom so he could by an apple for 2.75 + .50 = 3.25 (.25 goes to the company). Since I gave Tom .50 of value, I will get a percentage back on the next money he spends until it matches an interest set (maybe per commodity) on his next expenditures. So if he goes buy a hammer for $5, he might be charged $5.15, and I immediately get the .15. So on with future purchases of his until I get back my .50 + interest. Initially, since he is poor, my interest charges are small, but as he earns more and more (by spending other's subsidies on capital or education), the charges rise to match his socioeconomic status. If the rate is set ethically, he will now be way better off economically, which means he can subsidize other people.

This is all done completely automatically by the [[blockchain]], since you can track who transacted exactly how much to who else, and match the subsidizer and subsidee in one block of the ledger. Then, track wallet IDs to automatically deduct and deposit balances. 

The effective [[incentive structure]] of such a system is that the "rich" are incentivized to *invest* in the "poors" potential for economic success. The interest rate would have to be set in an optimal gradient such that it is liberating at first (very small payments), but not so big at the end that it traps rising stakeholders. Since the investing happens automatically on everyday purchases as well, people have more freedom to control their spending and traditional economic growth happens from profitability (in fact it is maximized since companies capture all possible prices).
