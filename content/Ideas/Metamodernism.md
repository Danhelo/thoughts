Look for [[Dave Shapiro YT's]] explanation.

But from what I understood, it's the philosophical movement concept that asserts that we must move away from the two meaning deconstructing approaches of Western thought's last 500 years.

Modernism sought to find the truth out there and rationalize it, basing itself on Big God thinking and theology of medieval ages. (top down approach)

Postmodernism sought to deconstruct all truth, basing its assumptions on Nihilism and Nietzche. (relativistic or denial approach). It has led to a societal [[Crisis of Meaning]], and a corresponding [[Meaning Economy]] to meet the need. 

Metamodernism stands in opposition, proposing a bottom up approach. It recognizes that truth can't be directly captured but that it does in fact exist. The primordial substrate of reality exists and thus the truth around it can be encircled and triangulated, if not directly captured.

To me this is very interesting because it has to do with my own personal view on the overall structure of reality. It's no coincidence that the most exciting intellectual movements in physics, CS, and mathematics all follow this general approach. It's like if the intellectual paradigm of the time (here metamodernism) influences the sciences and art.

For example, I believe in the [[Hypergraphical Universe]], as explained by [[Stephen Wolfram]] and [[Joscha Bach]]. It is structured in the same way, with a fundamental informational substrate, and computational processes (information) built in increasing abstraction layers that we approximate through science and philosophy. Exactly that is the approach to truth that metamodernism proposes. Interesting. We are in the midst of the start of an intellectual revolution.
