In [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"), a **hypergraph** is a generalization of a [graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_(discrete_mathematics) "Graph (discrete mathematics)") in which an [edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_theory "Graph theory") can join any number of [vertices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_(graph_theory) "Vertex (graph theory)"). In contrast, in an ordinary graph, an edge connects exactly two vertices.

While graph edges connect only 2 nodes, hyperedges connect an arbitrary number of nodes. However, it is often desirable to study hypergraphs where all hyperedges have the same cardinality; a _k-uniform hypergraph_ is a hypergraph such that all its hyperedges have size _k_. (In other words, one such hypergraph is a collection of sets, each such set a hyperedge connecting _k_ nodes.) So a 2-uniform hypergraph is a graph, a 3-uniform hypergraph is a collection of unordered triples, and so on. An undirected hypergraph is also called a _set system_ or a _[family of sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_of_sets "Family of sets")_ drawn from the [universal set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_set "Universal set").

Hypergraphs can be viewed as [incidence structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incidence_structure "Incidence structure"). In particular, there is a bipartite "incidence graph" or "[Levi graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_graph "Levi graph")" corresponding to every hypergraph, and conversely, every [bipartite graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartite_graph "Bipartite graph") can be regarded as the incidence graph of a hypergraph when it is 2-colored and it is indicated which color class corresponds to hypergraph vertices and which to hypergraph edges.

Hypergraphs have many other names. In [computational geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_geometry "Computational geometry"), an undirected hypergraph may sometimes be called a **range space** and then the hyperedges are called _ranges_.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergraph#cite_note-2) In [cooperative game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_game_theory "Cooperative game theory") theory, hypergraphs are called **simple games** (voting games); this notion is applied to solve problems in [social choice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_choice_theory "Social choice theory"). In some literature edges are referred to as _hyperlinks_ or _connectors_.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergraph#cite_note-3)

The collection of hypergraphs is a [category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_(mathematics) "Category (mathematics)") with hypergraph [homomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homomorphism "Homomorphism") as [morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphism "Morphism").

## Generalizations of concepts from graphs[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hypergraph&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Generalizations of concepts from graphs")]

Many [theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theorem "Theorem") and concepts involving graphs also hold for hypergraphs, in particular:

- [Matching in hypergraphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matching_in_hypergraphs "Matching in hypergraphs");
- [Vertex cover in hypergraphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_cover_in_hypergraphs "Vertex cover in hypergraphs") (also known as: **transversal**);
- [Line graph of a hypergraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_graph_of_a_hypergraph "Line graph of a hypergraph");
- [Hypergraph grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergraph_grammar "Hypergraph grammar") - created by augmenting a class of hypergraphs with a set of replacement rules;
- [Ramsey's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey%27s_theorem "Ramsey's theorem");
- [Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erd%C5%91s%E2%80%93Ko%E2%80%93Rado_theorem "Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem");
- [Kruskal–Katona theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruskal%E2%80%93Katona_theorem "Kruskal–Katona theorem") on uniform hypergraphs;
- [Hall-type theorems for hypergraphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall-type_theorems_for_hypergraphs "Hall-type theorems for hypergraphs").

In directed hypergraphs: [transitive closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_closure "Transitive closure"), and shortest path problems.
